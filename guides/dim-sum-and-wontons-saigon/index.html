Dim sum in Saigon: dumplings, wontons and egg noodles in Cholon

Short answer: if you only have time for a few, start with the spots local creators' videos have been watched most: Shan Dimsum (dim sum), Thiem Huy Noodles (egg noodles & wontons) and Kieu Ky Har Gow (dim sum). Every place below shows its Vietnamese name, address and directions.

Cantonese and Teochew families brought dim sum, wontons and springy egg noodles to Cholon, and the old shops still make them by hand. In Vietnamese you will see há cảo (har gow), xíu mại (siu mai), sủi cảo (wonton dumplings) and mì (egg noodles).

Dim sum here is a morning meal: go early for the freshest steamers. Wonton and egg-noodle shops open from breakfast until they sell out, and some carts only appear in the evening.

Before you go

  • Point at what other tables are eating; steamers are usually priced per basket.
  • Ask for 'mì khô' for dry egg noodles with soup on the side.
  • Cash is the norm at older shops.
