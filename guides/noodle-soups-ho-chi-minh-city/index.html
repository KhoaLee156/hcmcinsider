Where to eat noodle soup in Saigon: hu tieu, duck noodles, pho and more

Short answer: if you only have time for a few, start with the spots local creators' videos have been watched most: 8,000-dong Noodle Cart (hu tieu noodle soup), Thiem Huy Noodles (egg noodles & wontons) and My Kim Noodles (hu tieu noodle soup). Every place below shows its Vietnamese name, address and directions.

Noodle soup is breakfast, lunch and late-night food in Saigon. Pho gets the fame, but locals are just as loyal to hu tieu (a clear pork-and-seafood broth with rice noodles, often in Phnom Penh style), Chinese egg noodles with wontons, herbal duck noodles and spicy Hue-style beef noodles.

These are shops local food creators keep coming back to, from an 8,000-dong cart to old family shops in Cholon. Prices are low, portions are made to order, and most places add fresh herbs, lime and chilli at the table.

Before you go

  • Order 'kho' (dry) to get the noodles with sauce and the broth on the side.
  • Fresh herbs and chilli on the table are free; taste the broth before you add sauces.
  • Popular carts sell out early, so go before noon for morning-only places.

First time eating your way around Saigon?

A local-led street food tour (on foot or on the back of a motorbike) is an easy first night: someone orders for you and explains every dish. Then come back to the places below on your own.
