Hỷ Ký: chicken noodles with xá kén in Phú Bình

A morning-only chicken noodle shop in a Phú Bình alley, where the dipping bowl of xá kén is the real star. 50,000 dong a bowl.

By HCMC Insider · 30 October 2026

I ate at Mì gà Hỷ Ký, a long-running shop in the Phú Bình parish area of old District 11. A normal bowl of chicken noodles with xá kén costs 50,000 dong.

The bowl arrives neat: a clear, golden broth, finely chopped spring onion, yellow egg noodles and bean sprouts at the bottom. Lift the chicken and the skin is yellow with tiny dimples, over a thin ivory layer. The meat is tender and fragrant, and even before any sauce you can clearly taste the chicken.

Then comes the dipping bowl. Chopped xá kén sits in a whole steel bowl on the table; you spoon some into your own dish, add chilli sauce, a few slices of green chilli and a splash of the dark brown dipping sauce. Dip the chicken in and it is hot and pungent, nothing like the ginger fish sauce or salt, pepper and lime I usually have with boiled chicken. Eat it once and you remember it; it doesn't taste like anything else.

From what I've read, xá kén is a kind of root and is considered the soul of this dish. And yellow, dimpled skin like this, as far as I know, usually means a free-range local chicken (gà ta).

The Phú Bình housing area is said to be the capital of chicken noodles with xá kén. Going there myself, I counted about five or six shops. I've eaten at quite a few, and Hỷ Ký is still the one I think is most worth it.

Beyond the taste, the shop is very clean. The young owner has a great memory: he takes orders without writing anything down and works fast.

The downside is seating. The shop is in a narrow alley; come late and it's full, with no tables and a long wait. It sells in the morning and usually runs out by around 11 am, so go early.

You can also order a quarter or half chicken, and choose thigh, wing, back, upper thigh, feet or neck. Try a bite of chicken without sauce first to taste the bird, then move on to the xá kén. If you can't take much heat, go easy on it.

Verdict: worth the money, as long as you're willing to get up early. What do you usually dip boiled chicken in?

Practical info: Mì gà Hỷ Ký, Phú Bình parish area, old District 11, Ho Chi Minh City. 50,000 dong per normal bowl; quarter and half chickens available. Mornings only, usually sold out by about 11 am.
