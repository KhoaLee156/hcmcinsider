Riding Saigon's Metro Line 1: Ben Thanh to Thu Duc and back

A real ride on Metro Line 1: buying a ticket at Ben Thanh, what it cost (29,000 dong with deposit), getting the deposit back at Thu Duc, and tips for visitors.

By HCMC Insider · 14 November 2026

Metro Line 1 is the easiest way to get from the city centre out to the east of Saigon without sitting in traffic. I rode it on a Saturday morning in November, from Ben Thanh to Thu Duc and back, and photographed every step so you know exactly what to expect.

Ben Thanh station is underground, right next to Ben Thanh Market. The passages are wide, air-conditioned and well signed in Vietnamese and English, and every exit is numbered with the streets it leads to.

There are three ways to pay. The fastest is to tap a contactless Visa or Mastercard, or scan an e-wallet QR code, straight at the gate. You can also buy a QR ticket at a kiosk by card. The classic ticket machines take cash only.

I used a ticket machine to see how it works. You pick your destination on the line map, and the screen shows the fare. Ben Thanh to Thu Duc was 14,000 dong, plus a 15,000 dong deposit for the plastic card: 29,000 dong in total. The machine takes notes from 1,000 dong up; it did not take bank cards.

Tap the card at the gate and head down to the platform. The trains are clean, cool and quiet, with announcements and screens in English. From tapping in at Ben Thanh to stepping out on the platform at Thu Duc took about 25 minutes, including the wait.

After the underground stretch, the line rises onto a viaduct and the stations are elevated, with long covered platforms and screen doors. Thu Duc is an ordinary neighbourhood station, a good way to see everyday life on the east side of the city.

The tip most visitors miss: keep your single-journey card, because you need it to exit, and then put it back into a ticket machine at your destination using the card return option. The machine gives you your 15,000 dong deposit back in cash.

For the ride back, follow the signs for platform 1 towards Ben Thanh. Rush hours are busy, but on a weekend morning there was plenty of room.

If you go: the line runs from about 5 am to 10 pm on weekdays and until 11 pm at weekends. Have small notes ready if you use the machines, or simply tap a contactless bank card at the gate. Full details on stations, fares and day passes are in the HCMC Insider metro guide.
