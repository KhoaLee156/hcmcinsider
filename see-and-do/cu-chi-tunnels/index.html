Cu Chi Tunnels

Địa đạo Củ Chi

Is it worth visiting? Yes, if you want to understand the war from the Vietnamese side. The tunnels are about 70 km from the city centre, so the real question is how you get there: a half-day tour is easiest, the public bus is cheap but slow. There are two sites, Ben Dinh (closer, busier) and Ben Duoc (further, quieter).

Why go

  • A network of about 250 km of tunnels dug from 1948 and joined into one system in the 1960s, with kitchens, meeting rooms and field hospitals underground.
  • You can crawl through a short, widened section, see hidden trapdoors and booby traps, and taste steamed cassava, the wartime staple.
  • It is one of Vietnam's most visited sites and ranks among Tripadvisor's top attractions in Asia (CafeF, June 2026).

How to get there

  • Half-day tour from District 1 (by bus, or by speedboat on the Saigon River): the easiest option, ticket and guide usually included.
  • Public bus to Ben Duoc: bus 13 from Saigon bus station to Cu Chi bus station, then bus 79. Allow 2+ hours each way; city buses are free until 31 Dec 2026.
  • From Cholon: bus 94 from Cholon bus station to Cu Chi, then bus 79.
  • Ride-hailing car: about 1.5–2 hours each way depending on traffic; agree on a wait-and-return if you book by the hour.

Local tips

  • Start early: mornings are cooler and the busiest tour groups arrive late morning.
  • Wear clothes you don't mind getting dusty, closed shoes and insect repellent.
  • The tunnels are tight and dark: skip the crawl if you are claustrophobic; there is plenty to see above ground.
  • A shooting range on site sells bullets for real wartime rifles; it is loud and optional.
